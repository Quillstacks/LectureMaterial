\subsection{Business and ML Objectives}

\begin{frame}{System Design}
  \sidedrawing{IEb}
  \begin{sidebody}
    ML systems design takes a \alertbf{system approach} to MLOps: the business
    requirements, the data stack, infrastructure, deployment, monitoring, and
    their stakeholders work together to satisfy the specified objectives and
    requirements.
    \vskip0.8em
    If this system is built for a business, it must be driven by business
    objectives, which will need to be translated into ML objectives to guide
    the development of ML models.
  \end{sidebody}
  \slidesources{Huyen2022}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Tie ML to business}
  \sidedrawing{IEd}
  \begin{sidebody}
    For an ML project to succeed within a business organization, it's crucial
    to tie the performance of an ML system to the overall business
    performance. At best the ML metric becomes a \textbf{proxy} for a
    business metric.
    \vskip0.8em
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Ad click-through rate} every increase results in actual
        ad revenue.
      \item \textbf{Fraud detection} every fraudulent transaction stopped
        results in actual money saved.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{sidebody}
  \slidesources{Huyen2022}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Netflix take-rate}
  \begin{withmargin}
    \begin{tdmain}
      Netflix measures the performance of their recommender system using
      take-rate.
      \[
        \text{take-rate} \;=\;
        \frac{\textcolor{DHBWred}{\text{quality plays}}}
             {\text{recommendations seen}}
      \]
      The higher the take-rate, the better the recommender system.
    \end{tdmain}
    \begin{tdmargin}
      Put in context with total streaming hours and the subscription
      cancellation rate: a higher take-rate also results in more streaming
      hours and fewer cancellations.
      \vskip0.5em
      Over years of personalization, monthly churn fell by several percentage
      points, worth more than \$1B per year~\cite{GomezUribe2015}.
    \end{tdmargin}
  \end{withmargin}
  \slidesources{Chandrashekar2017,GomezUribe2015}
\end{frame}

\subsection{Requirements for ML Systems}

\begin{frame}{Forty-two}
  % Asset brief: Deep Thought announcing forty-two, the answer seven and a half
  % million years in the making, to a crowd that never knew the question.
  \phimgside[0.33]{deep_thought_42.png}{Deep Thought: forty-two}
  \begin{sidebody}
    ``I think the problem, to be quite honest with you, is that you've never
    actually known what the question is.''
    \vskip1.0em
    We can't say that we've successfully built an ML system without knowing
    what \alertbf{requirements} the system has to satisfy.
    \vskip0.6em
    They vary from use case to use case. Most systems should have four:
    reliability, scalability, maintainability, and adaptability.
  \end{sidebody}
  \slidesources{Adams1979,Huyen2022}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Reliability}
  \begin{withmargin}
    \begin{tdmain}
      \begin{tddef}{Reliability}
        The system should continue to perform the correct function at the
        desired level of performance even in the face of adversity: hardware
        or software faults, and even human error.
      \end{tddef}
    \end{tdmain}
    \begin{tdmargin}
      Traditional software warns you: a crash, a runtime error, a 404.
      \vskip0.5em
      ML systems can fail silently. \texttt{model.predict()} is called
      correctly, but the predictions are wrong, and without ground truth labels
      nobody knows. Translate a sentence into a language you don't know.
    \end{tdmargin}
  \end{withmargin}
  \slidesources{Kleppmann2017,Huyen2022}
\end{frame}


% Copy then extend: the same definition, the margin turns from the failure to
% the design answer to it.
\begin{frame}{Reliability}
  \begin{withmargin}
    \begin{tdmain}
      \begin{tddef}{Reliability}
        The system should continue to perform the correct function at the
        desired level of performance even in the face of adversity: hardware
        or software faults, and even human error.
      \end{tddef}
    \end{tdmain}
    \begin{tdmargin}
      \textbf{Fail loudly.} A fault surfaces immediately and visibly, where it
      happens, instead of carrying on with bad state.
      \vskip0.5em
      \textbf{Degrade gracefully.} Under overload, lower the quality of the
      response rather than drop it: a smaller index, a cheaper ranking, a
      simpler model.
    \end{tdmargin}
  \end{withmargin}
  \slidesources{Shore2004,Beyer2016}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Scalability}
  Whichever way your system grows, there should be \alertbf{reasonable ways}
  of dealing with that growth.
  \vskip0.8em
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.31\textwidth}
      \begin{tdblockt}[coltitle=tdfg,before upper=\raggedright,equal height group=scale]{Complexity}
        Last year a logistic regression on 1 GB of RAM. This year a 100 million
        parameter network that needs 16 GB.
      \end{tdblockt}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.31\textwidth}
      \begin{tdblockt}[coltitle=tdfg,before upper=\raggedright,equal height group=scale]{Traffic volume}
        10{,}000 prediction requests daily at launch. Between 1 million and 10
        million once the user base grows.
      \end{tdblockt}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.31\textwidth}
      \begin{tdblockt}[coltitle=tdfg,before upper=\raggedright,equal height group=scale]{Model count}
        One model per enterprise customer. A startup Huyen worked with had
        8{,}000 models in production.
      \end{tdblockt}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \slidesources{Kleppmann2017,Huyen2022}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Scaling is not only resources}
  \begin{withmargin}
    \begin{tdmain}
      \textbf{Resource scaling.} At peak 100 GPUs, most of the time 10.
      Autoscaling scales up and down with usage, and is tricky to implement.
      \vskip0.8em
      \textbf{\alertbf{Artifact management.}} One model you can monitor and
      retrain by hand. With one hundred, monitoring and retraining need to be
      automated, and every model reproducible.
    \end{tdmain}
    \begin{tdmargin}
      Even Amazon fell victim: autoscaling failed on Prime Day 2018 and the
      system crashed.
      \vskip0.5em
      One hour of downtime was estimated to cost Amazon between \$72 million
      and \$99 million.
    \end{tdmargin}
  \end{withmargin}
  \slidesources{Wolfe2018,Huyen2022}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Maintainability}
  \begin{withmargin}
    \begin{tdmain}
      ML engineers, DevOps engineers, and subject matter experts, from very
      different backgrounds, own different parts of the process.
      \vskip0.6em
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Everyone works with tools they are comfortable with
        \item Code is documented
        \item Code, data, and artifacts are \alertbf{versioned}
        \item Models are reproducible when the original authors are not around
        \item Problems get solved without finger pointing
      \end{itemize}
    \end{tdmain}
    \begin{tdmargin}
      ``Over time, many different people will work on the system \ldots\ and
      they should all be able to work on it productively.''
    \end{tdmargin}
  \end{withmargin}
  \slidesources{Kleppmann2017,Huyen2022}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Adaptability}
  \begin{tddef}{Adaptability}
    To adapt to shifting data distributions and business requirements, the
    system should have some capacity for both discovering aspects for
    performance improvement and allowing updates without service interruption.
  \end{tddef}
  \vskip1.0em
  ML systems are part code, part data, and data can change quickly.
  \vskip0.4em
  {\color{tddim}Lecture VIII, Data Distribution Shifts and
  Monitoring. Lecture IX, Continual Learning and Test in Production.}
  \slidesources{Huyen2022}
\end{frame}


% Not one of Huyen's four. Added because LLMs and agents take over decisions,
% and at that scale the question is no longer only whether the system works.
\begin{frame}{Alignment and Controllability}
  \begin{withmargin}
    \begin{tdmain}
      \begin{tdblockt}[coltitle=tdfg]{Alignment}
        Make AI systems behave in line with human intentions and values.
      \end{tdblockt}
      \vskip0.5em
      \begin{tdblockt}[coltitle=tdfg]{Controllability}
        Humans can supervise the system and intervene when it deviates from
        the expected behavior.
      \end{tdblockt}
    \end{tdmain}
    \begin{tdmargin}
      Robustness, Interpretability, \alertbf{Controllability}, Ethicality: the
      four objectives of alignment.
      \vskip0.5em
      LLMs and agents take over decision making, or at least augment it.
    \end{tdmargin}
  \end{withmargin}
  \slidesources{Ji2023}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{In the loop, on the loop}
  \begin{withmargin}
    \begin{tdmain}
      Controllability is a design decision: \alertbf{who decides}, and who
      acts.
      \vskip1.0em
      \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
      {\centering
        \begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
          \toprule
          \textbf{Human} & \textbf{Decides} & \textbf{Acts}\\
          \midrule
          in the loop     & human, on a proposal & system\\
          on the loop     & system, human can veto & system\\
          out of the loop & system & system\\
          \bottomrule
        \end{tabular}\par}
    \end{tdmain}
    \begin{tdmargin}
      Served to a lot of users, scale, bias and performative prediction
      become requirements.
      \vskip0.5em
      Interpretability and the human machine interface decide whether the
      human can intervene at all. Lecture I: human on the loop.
    \end{tdmargin}
  \end{withmargin}
  \slidesources{Parasuraman2000,Perdomo2020}
\end{frame}
